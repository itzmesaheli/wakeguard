    Head and attention

    Press Calibrate while looking straight ahead. It sets your normal head position.

    Alerts
    Uses the microphone and your browser's speech service. Works in Chrome. You will be asked for permission.
    Driving timer

    The timer counts from when you press Start. Experts advise a 15 to 20 minute break about every 2 hours, even if you feel fine.

    Emergency contact

    After repeated alarms, a "Take a break" message appears with a button to call this person. The number is saved only in this browser.

    Drivers

    Each driver has their own settings, calibration, history and alarm log. Switch drivers before you press Start.

    Backup and restore

    Saves your sessions, alarm log, settings and drivers in one file. Importing adds to what you already have.

    How it works and tips

    WakeGuard uses your camera to find points on your face. It measures how open your eyes and mouth are and how far your head is tilted or turned. If your eyes stay closed too long, your head drops, or you look away from the road, it sounds an alarm. A soft beep comes first when tiredness is building.

    Camera placement

    • Put the phone or laptop at eye level, straight in front of you, about an arm's length away.
    • Keep your whole face in view with both eyes visible. The steering wheel or your hand should not cover it.
    • Use a mount so the camera stays steady.

    Lighting

    • Light should fall on your face, not come from behind you. A bright window or the sun behind your head makes your face dark.
    • At night, turn on a dim cabin light. Avoid headlights shining straight into the lens.
    • Take off sunglasses. They hide your eyes, and WakeGuard will warn you if it cannot see them.

    Calibration

    • Start runs a short 3-step calibration. Sit as you normally drive and look straight at the road.
    • Press Calibrate again if you move the camera, change your seat or put on glasses.
    • Check Head tilt: it should be near 0° when you look ahead and go positive when your head drops forward.

    Good habits

    • Take a 15 to 20 minute break about every 2 hours, even if you feel fine.
    • If alarms keep going off, stop and rest. Coffee only helps for a short time.
    • Press Test alarm sound and Test voice before you drive.
    • This is a helper, not a safety device.
    This is a learning project, not a safety device. It can miss drowsiness (sunglasses, poor light, camera angle) and can raise false alarms. If you feel sleepy, stop driving and rest. Do not rely on this to stay awake.
